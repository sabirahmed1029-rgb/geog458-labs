1.
I chose the San Francisco Bay Area in California because it is an important location for vessel traffic, especially with the Golden Gate Bridge and the surrounding waterways. I was interested in seeing how ships move in and out of the Bay Area and how cargo vessels help transport goods and supplies. I thought it would be interesting to see the different vessel routes and understand how important this traffic is to the movement of goods in the region. I selected April 29–30, 2025, for my data collection. I used vessel traffic data from AccessAIS. I chose a short two-day period and a specific geographic area to keep the dataset manageable while still being able to observe vessel movement throughout the region.

2.
When looking at the map, I noticed that different vessel types had different movement patterns. Cargo vessels mostly traveled toward the northwest side of San Francisco Bay, and tankers appeared to follow somewhat similar routes. Passenger vessels seemed to have more movement across the region, traveling between different areas such as Oakland, Berkeley, and San Rafael. Their paths appeared more spread out rather than following one main route. I also noticed that passenger vessels seemed to have more activity and sometimes crossed paths with cargo and tanker vessels. In terms of timing, passenger vessels appeared to be active earlier in the day. Cargo vessels seemed to start off with less activity, with more movement appearing later in the day. Tankers also appeared to have activity earlier in the day. Overall, the map helped me see how different types of vessels move throughout the Bay Area and how their routes can overlap.


3.
The source I found was [MyShipTracking](https://www.myshiptracking.com/), which gave me some new ideas for features we could add to our map. One thing that stood out to me was how much information it provides about individual vessels, such as the vessel's build, size, speed, current status, voyage, departure location, and destination. I think adding some of these details to our map would make it more useful because right now our map mainly shows where vessels are moving and the routes they are taking. It would be interesting if users could click on a vessel and see more information about it, including where its journey started, where it is heading, and the approximate time it takes to get there. It would also be helpful to see what kind of cargo the vessel is carrying and when it is expected to arrive, if that information is available. I think this map could be useful for people who don't really understand how vessel traffic and cargo shipping work because it would give them a better idea of how goods are transported. It could also be useful for shipping companies that want to monitor their vessels and keep track of their routes and voyages.
